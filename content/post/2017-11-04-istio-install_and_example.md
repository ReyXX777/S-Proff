## The Morning Hustle
![screenshot](/img/mask.jpg)

The day began at 6:00 AM. I packed my bag with my trusty stethoscope, surgical cap, and a notebook to jot down everything I learned. By 7:00 AM, I was in the hospital, scrubbing in for the morning ward rounds. The surgery department was buzzing with activity—nurses preparing instruments, residents reviewing patient charts, and consultants discussing the day’s cases.

During the rounds, I observed how the team meticulously reviewed each patient’s progress. From post-operative care to managing complications, every detail mattered. I was struck by how the surgeons balanced clinical expertise with empathy, reassuring patients and their families with a calm demeanor.


## The First Case: A Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy

The highlight of the day was assisting in a laparoscopic cholecystectomy (gallbladder removal). As I stood in the operating theater, dressed in scrubs and a surgical mask, I felt a mix of excitement and nervousness. The atmosphere was intense but focused.

The surgeon walked me through the procedure, explaining each step:

Port placement: How tiny incisions were made for the camera and instruments.

Visualization: The gallbladder appeared on the monitor, magnified and clear.

Dissection: The careful separation of the gallbladder from surrounding tissues.

Removal: The gallbladder was extracted through one of the small incisions.

I was amazed by the precision and coordination of the team. The anesthesiologist monitored the patient’s vitals, the nurses handed instruments with clockwork efficiency, and the surgeon operated with steady hands. It was like watching a well-choreographed dance.
### The Learning Curve
One of the most valuable lessons I learned was the importance of sterility. From scrubbing in to maintaining a sterile field, every step was crucial to prevent infections. I also realized how much preparation goes into surgery—pre-operative assessments, imaging studies, and patient consent are all part of the process.

During the procedure, the surgeon quizzed me on anatomy and surgical principles. It was both challenging and rewarding to apply my textbook knowledge to real-life scenarios. I also learned about post-operative care, including pain management and monitoring for complications.

### The Human Side of Surgery

What struck me the most was the human connection in surgery. Before the procedure, the surgeon took time to explain the surgery to the patient, addressing their concerns and calming their nerves. After the surgery, the team celebrated the successful outcome, reminding me that behind every case is a person trusting us with their life.

### Reflections on the Day
By the end of the day, I was exhausted but exhilarated. My first surgery rotation taught me more than I could have imagined—not just about surgical techniques, but also about teamwork, communication, and the art of healing.

Surgery is more than just cutting and stitching; it’s about precision, patience, and compassion. As I left the hospital that evening, I felt a renewed sense of purpose. This experience solidified my passion for medicine and inspired me to work harder, learn more, and one day, make a difference in the operating room.


### Tips for Medical Students Starting Their Surgery Rotation

````toml
Be Prepared: Review anatomy, surgical procedures, and common conditions before your rotation.

Ask Questions: Don’t hesitate to ask for explanations or clarifications.

Stay Curious: Observe everything—from patient interactions to surgical techniques.

Practice Sterility: Learn and follow sterile protocols meticulously.

Reflect: Take time to process your experiences and learn from them

````

#### Surgery is a challenging but incredibly rewarding field, and my first rotation was a glimpse into the dedication and skill it demands. To all my fellow medical students, embrace every moment of your rotations—it’s where textbooks come to life, and your journey as a healer truly begins.
